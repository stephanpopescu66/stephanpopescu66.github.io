Custom Electric Guitar

Four guitars.
Sixteen weeks.

In a 4-person design group, the goal was to design and build a 6-string electric guitar from scratch while learning stringed-instrument theory and best practices for precision wood manufacturing.

4playable guitars from 1 shared design, built like a small production run
2 pickupswith a 3-way switch, plus volume and tone knobs
MapleCNC-contoured neck and body, laser-cut acrylic pickguard
How we built it

Deliverables were a Gantt chart, a per-guitar budget, and 4 playable guitars that shared 1 design, so we got real experience with a mass-manufacturing process. My role was to design and make the neck for each guitar, and I also helped design and make the body.

Prototypes. We cut the first parts from foam-board body blanks and pine neck blanks. In V-Carve, I programmed 2D and 3D toolpaths with surface finish in mind. Getting the toolpaths right was the slowest part, because every production part had to be identical. We laser-cut a cardboard pickguard to check fit and style.

Bodies. The CNC cut the pockets. A hand rasp shaped the body and arm cutouts, an edge router made the edge radius, and then we did many rounds of sanding and clear coat.

Necks. These were harder, because the stock had to be machined from both sides. I added 2 alignment pins so we could find zero again after flipping it. The CNC cut the back profile, truss-rod slot, headstock, and tuner holes. With a ½ in ball end mill, the cycle times were very long, so I made the stepover bigger and smoothed the neck by hand. Then we put in the frets and trimmed them.

Finish. We laser-cut the acrylic pickguards, wired the pickups to the switch and knobs, and then installed hardware, tuned, intonated, and played. Mind the high E string. I snapped it while intonating.

Stock: 4 × 30 × 0.75 in maple (neck), 15 × 20 × 1.5 in maple (body). Machines: planer, CNC gantry router, fillet router, laser cutter.

Manufacturing Systems

Metal hammer.

A 6061 aluminum handle turned on the manual and CNC lathe, and a 1018 hot-rolled steel head made on CNC and manual mills. I wrote the G-code to engrave our initials on the 5-axis Hurco.

Learn more

Handle: 6061 aluminum, 1 in round stock. Horizontal bandsaw, manual lathe, CNC lathe.

Head: 1018 hot-rolled steel. Horizontal grinder, 5-axis CNC mill, manual mill.

Engraving: The program used machine commands (M3, M5, M8, M10) and G-code for circular interpolation, absolute and incremental positioning, and linear moves in XYZ (G90, G91, G01, G02, G03).

Research

Additive manufacturing research.

As an undergraduate research assistant, I tested an algorithm that corrects print distortion and poor quality, and compared the results to control prints.

Learn more

I designed simple and complex test geometry and built a library of parts for distortion and surface-finish tests. For each part I made a detailed drawing with its critical dimensions, then measured the prints with a digital caliper and a 3D surface profiler. I used a SolidWorks design table driven from Excel to control revisions, so I changed dimensions in one sheet and not by hand.
